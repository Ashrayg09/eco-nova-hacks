What is it

About the event

Eco Nova Hacks is a two-day hackathon for students who care about the environment. You'll form a team, pick a problem worth solving, and build a real sustainability project over the weekend, then show it to judges on Sunday. Bring a laptop and an idea; we'll handle the food, space, and mentors.

Why join

What makes Eco Nova Hacks special

Climate-first

Every project tackles a real environmental problem: energy, waste, water, or climate. If it helps the planet, it fits.

No experience needed

Never been to a hackathon? Good, plenty of people here haven't either. Saturday-morning workshops get you building fast.

Build something real

Use actual tools and APIs to ship a working demo over the weekend, with no toy examples, whatever your experience level.

Prizes worth winning

Gift cards for the top three teams, judged on real impact, how it's built, and design.

Mentors on hand

People from climate orgs and tech companies are around all day, so ask them anything, from first ideas to last-minute debugging.

Meet your people

Spend a weekend with other students who care about this, swap ideas, and leave with people worth knowing.

Five tracks

Tracks

Pick one to focus on, or combine a few. Build whatever fits.

Clean Energy

Smarter grids, solar tooling, energy-use dashboards, and efficiency hacks.

Oceans & Water

Marine health, water quality, conservation data, and coastal resilience.

Waste & Circularity

Recycling, reuse marketplaces, food-waste reduction, and circular supply chains.

Green Mobility

Public transit, bike-share, EV tooling, and low-carbon logistics.

Climate Awareness

Education, carbon tracking, and tools that turn data into action.

Good to know

FAQ

Who can attend?

Any current high-school or college student, regardless of major or experience. First-timers are especially welcome.

How much does it cost?

Nothing. Eco Nova Hacks is 100% free, including meals, snacks, and swag, thanks to our sponsors.

Do I need a team?

Nope. Come solo or with friends. We'll run a team-formation session at the start so no one hacks alone.

What should I bring?

Your laptop and charger, a student ID, and anything you need to stay comfy overnight (a hoodie, toiletries, maybe a sleeping bag).

Do I need to know how to code?

Not necessarily. We welcome designers, researchers, and idea people. Workshops and mentors will help you learn as you build.

Is it in person?

It's hybrid. Day one (Sat, Nov 21) is in person at the San Diego Central Library, and day two (Sun, Nov 22), with submissions, pitches, and awards, happens online over Zoom.
